# Assets: pictures, footage, textures, fonts, maps, and the licences behind them

Vox's rule: collect the footage, documents and photos **before** you finish the script, and write around them (Phil Edwards). Find the "Rosetta stone" document that carries the story (S). An explainer is only as good as its archive.

## Archives (photos, film, documents)

| Source | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [NASA Image and Video Library](https://images.nasa.gov) | "Generally not subject to copyright in the United States" | No implied endorsement; NASA logos and insignia are protected; identifiable people may need permission for commercial use. The demo uses four NASA photos. |
| [Library of Congress, Free to Use and Reuse](https://www.loc.gov/free-to-use/) | Rights-free sets | Some items credit other sources; check each one. |
| [US National Archives](https://www.archives.gov) | NARA-made works are public domain | NARA "cannot confirm copyright status" for donated holdings; credit the source. |
| [Prelinger Archives](https://archive.org/details/prelinger) | Free to download and reuse | Classic mid-century newsreel and ephemeral film. |
| [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org) | Per-file licence (PD, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA) | Read every file's licence box; CC BY-SA means your video inherits it. |
| [Smithsonian Open Access](https://www.si.edu/openaccess) | CC0 items are marked | Filter by CC0. |
| British Pathé | **Not free** | A licence is needed even for non-commercial online use. |

Practical tips:
- Download the largest size. Cards get pushed 6–15%, so a 1080-px image is too small for a full-frame card.
- Keep a `SOURCES.md` next to every project: file, source URL, licence, credit line.
- Treat graphic or traumatic real photos with care. Vox's animation team rarely uses them (S).
- Label borrowed footage on screen ("Archive · NASA") with `label(..., { style: "paper" })`.

## Textures

Everything in `kit/textures/` is **generated by `scripts/make_textures.py`** (seeded, no downloads), so it is MIT like the rest of the repo: paper, film grain (8 tiles), dust (12 frames), crumpled paper, masking tape, halftone dots.

If you want scanned textures:
- **Texturelabs** is free for commercial use with no credit, BUT **no redistribution** (not even free, not inside a template or a repo) and no AI training. Use it in your own videos; never commit it to a shared repo.
- Scan your own: a sheet of printer paper, a newspaper, kraft paper, masking tape at 600 dpi. Ten minutes gives you a texture library nobody else has.

| Tool | Licence | Note |

| [LaMa inpainting](https://github.com/advimman/lama) | Apache-2.0 | Fill the hole behind a cut-out before you parallax it. |

Then: `cutout(scene, "subject.png", { outline: 9 })` adds the sticker edge and shadow.

## Maps

`scripts/map.mjs` projects **Natural Earth** country shapes (public domain, via the `world-atlas` npm package, ISC) with `d3-geo` (ISC). It writes a JS file you load in the page. Countries, a great-circle route and named points stay separate, so the kit can fill, draw and pin them.

```
node scripts/map.mjs --name europe --size 1080x1300 --projection mercator --fit "France,Germany,Poland" \
     --highlight "Poland" --points "Warsaw:21.01,52.23;Berlin:13.40,52.52" --route "Berlin>Warsaw" --out my-video/map.js
```

Borders are Natural Earth's **de-facto** lines, not every country's official ones. **Check disputed borders yourself before you publish.** Vox has pulled videos over map errors (S).

> **Publishing for an Indian audience?** Natural Earth's default shapes split Kashmir and differ from India's official map (Kashmir, Ladakh, Arunachal Pradesh). Publishing a map of India that differs from the official boundary can be illegal in India, and platforms take such maps down. Use the Survey of India's official boundary, or Natural Earth's India point-of-view data (`ne_10m_admin_0_countries_ind`), or keep the frame off disputed areas. The same care applies to Crimea, Western Sahara, Taiwan, Palestine and others.

## Other tools worth knowing

- [HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes) (Apache-2.0) also renders HTML to MP4 and has a "vox-explainer" skill. This kit is standalone and focuses on the craft rules, sound and measured numbers. You can use the kit's CSS/JS inside HyperFrames compositions too.
- GSAP is free to use, including commercially, under its own ["Standard No Charge" licence](https://gsap.com/standard-license). It is not open source and has terms (for example, about tools that compete with Webflow), so read them. It is installed from npm and not bundled here.
- [Remotion](https://remotion.dev) if you prefer React. The look rules transfer one-to-one.
